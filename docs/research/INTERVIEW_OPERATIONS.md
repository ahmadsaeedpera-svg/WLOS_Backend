## 0. The thirty-second orientation

You are running 14 conversations with women about a recent week that went
badly. You are testing one thing: **when a week goes badly, is the cause one
problem or several problems interacting — and does she see it herself?**

Three rules make or break the study:

1. **You talk less than 20% of the time.** Your job is silence, not curiosity.
2. **If she names one cause, you do not hand her a second.** A "no" is the
   study's most valuable result (§3).
3. **You never mention the product before Section 7.** Doing so ends the
   validity of that interview.

Everything below is machinery for those three rules.

## 2. Follow-up rules

### 2.1 When you may speak

You may speak in exactly five situations:

| # | Situation | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opening a section | Read the protocol's section question **verbatim** |
| 2 | She has finished, and the section still has a gap | Ask **one** listed follow-up from that section — her words only |
| 3 | You genuinely did not hear or understand a word | *"Sorry — I missed that word, could you say it again?"* |
| 4 | She asks you a direct question | Answer briefly and truthfully, or deflect per §2.4 |
| 5 | Distress, or a safeguarding disclosure | Stop the script. Go to §4 or §5 |

That is the whole list. Anything else is you filling silence.

### 2.2 When you must stay silent — the five-count

> **When she pauses, count to five before speaking.**

This is the protocol's rule, and it is the single highest-value behaviour in the
study. The protocol says it plainly: *the first silence is where the real answer
arrives.*

**How to actually do it under pressure:**

- Count **in your head, slowly: one … two … three … four … five.** Do not count
  on your fingers where she can see.
- Keep your face soft and still. Nodding is fine. **Do not say "mm-hm"** during
  the count — it is a prompt to continue, and it interrupts the very pause you
  are trying to create.
- If she is still silent at five, you may ask **one** listed follow-up from the
  current section. Not two. Not a compound.
- **If she resumes speaking at three, you have your data.** Say nothing. Let her
  run.

**The five-count is doubled in two places:**

| Place | Count | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Immediately after the §1 opening question | **Ten** | She is deciding which week to tell you about. Do not rescue her |
| After §6's *"Would you believe it was private?"* | **Hold it completely** | The protocol: *"Say nothing after. The pause is the measurement."* Her hesitation **is** the finding. Do not fill it, do not rephrase, do not offer an example. Wait for her to speak first |

**What silence feels like versus what it is.** Four seconds feels rude to you
and feels normal to her. You are the only person in the room who is
uncomfortable. That discomfort is the cost of the study.

### 2.3 Banned phrases, with the corrective for each

The protocol's §5 table is the authority. Here it is operationalised — the ban,
why it is banned, and **what to say instead**, so you have a move ready rather
than a prohibition.

| Never say | Why it destroys data | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| *"How do work, sleep and stress interact for you?"* | States the thesis out loud. Everything after it is contaminated — you have told her the answer you want | *"Tell me about the last week that went badly."* Then wait |
| *"Wouldn't it be useful if…"* | Leading. She will agree to be pleasant. Agreement is worthless | *"Was anything missing?"* |
| *"So what you're saying is…"* | Puts your framing in her mouth, then she confirms **your** sentence and you record it as hers | *"Can you say a bit more about that?"* — or say nothing at all |
| *"Most women tell us…"* | Social proof. She will move toward the group. Also breaks §9 — never tell a participant what other participants said | Say nothing. There is no acceptable version of this sentence |
| *"Do you track your cycle?"* | Imports the exact category the product is trying to escape, and tells her which app category you are in | *"Did you use anything — apps, notes, anything on your phone?"* |
| *"burnout"* / *"wellbeing"* / *"self-care"* | Your vocabulary, not hers. Once you say it, she will use it back and you cannot tell whose word it was | Use **only** the nouns she has already said. If she has not named it, it does not exist in this interview |
| Anything naming WLOS or the product before §7 | **Ends the study's validity** for that interview | §2.4 |

**Four more that are not in the protocol's table but break the same rules.**
Treat them as banned:

| Never say | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| *"Was that because of work?"* | Hands her a cause she did not name | *"What did you notice first?"* |
| *"That sounds exhausting / awful / really hard"* | Sympathy that names a state she did not. Warmth is fine; **labelling is not** | *"Thank you for telling me."* Or just wait |
| *"And did that affect your sleep?"* (when she has not mentioned sleep) | Introduces the second factor. **This is the §3 failure** | *"Was anything else going on that week?"* |
| *"Just to check I've got this right — it was X, then Y, then Z?"* | A summary is a leading question wearing a helmet. She will accept your chain | *"What happened next?"* Let her build the chain herself |

**If you slip.** Do not panic, do not apologise at length, do not try to undo
it. Say *"Sorry, ignore that — in your words, what happened?"*, move on, and
**write the exact words you used in the deviation box** (§6). A logged
contamination can be discounted at analysis. An unlogged one silently becomes a
finding.

### 2.4 If she asks what the product is

She may, especially the sceptics. Deflect **once**, warmly:

> *"There's genuinely nothing to show you — we're at the stage before that,
> which is why I'm just listening. Happy to tell you at the end if you're
> curious."*

Then return to the last question. **Deflect once only.** If she presses a second
time, tell her at the end and note it in the deviation box. (At screening, a
candidate who asks and cannot be redirected once is disqualified —
screener §5 — but a participant already in the room is never ejected for asking.)

### 2.5 Time discipline

45 minutes. Section budget, from protocol §4:

| §0 setup | §1 trigger | §2 interaction | §3 current solution | §4 failure | §5 return value | §6 employer | §7 reaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 min | 8 min | **10 min** | 7 min | 7 min | 7 min | 3 min | optional 3 min |

- If you are running long, **cut §7 first** — the protocol already discounts its
  value. Then trim §3.
- **Never cut §2 or §5.** §2 decides the study; §5 carries three of the five
  viability signals.
- Running over is her decision, not yours. Ask: *"We're at time — would you
  rather stop here, or is a few more minutes okay?"*

## 3. Section 2 — the critical discipline

**Read this before every single interview. It is the section that decides
everything, and it is the one you will get wrong under pressure.**

### 3.1 The rule

> **If she names ONE factor, do NOT introduce a second.**
>
> Ask: **"Was anything else going on that week?"**
>
> **ACCEPT NO.**

### 3.2 Why you will be tempted to break it

You will be sitting there, twelve minutes in, and she will have said *"work was
just really difficult."* One thing. No chain. And you will feel the study
failing in real time, and your mouth will want to say **"and how were you
sleeping?"**

That sentence would rescue the interview and destroy the study.

Because if you say it, one of two things happens. Either she says *"badly,
actually"* — and you have manufactured a two-factor interaction that did not
exist until you spoke it — or she says *"fine"* — and you have already told her
what you were fishing for, so everything she says afterwards is shaped by it.

**There is no version of introducing a second factor that produces usable data.**

### 3.3 Why a "no" is the most valuable answer in the study

The protocol is explicit: *"A no here is the most valuable data in the study —
it is the finding that falsifies the thesis, and it is worthless if the
interviewer talks her out of it."*

Understand what that means operationally:

- The thesis is that bad weeks are **interactions**, not single problems.
- The protocol's own kill condition is **Interaction = 0 in ≥ 10 of 14** →
  *change the product*.
- That kill condition can only ever fire if interviewers **let women say one
  thing and stop.**
- An interviewer who helps every participant find a second factor will produce
  14 interviews that appear to confirm the thesis and contain **no information
  whatsoever**. The study will have cost 14 women 45 minutes each and returned
  a number that was decided before fieldwork began.

**You are not failing when she says "no". You are the reason the study can
produce an answer at all.** A clean zero is a result. Record it with the same
satisfaction you would record a rich causal chain, because it is worth more.

### 3.4 The decision tree — run this in the room

```
She has finished her §1 account.
│
├─ She named TWO OR MORE factors (her own nouns)
│    → Use HER nouns only:
│      "You mentioned [her word], and you mentioned [her word].
│       Were those connected at all?"
│    → Then, as needed, one at a time:
│         "Which came first?"
│         "Did one make the other worse?"
│         "Has that combination happened before?"
│         "Is there a pattern you've noticed over time?"
│    → Record VERBATIM whether she:
│         volunteers the chain unprompted ........ Interaction 2
│         connects them only once you asked ...... Interaction 1
│         rejects the connection .................. Interaction 0
│
└─ She named ONE factor
     → Ask EXACTLY: "Was anything else going on that week?"
     → Five-count. Say nothing else.
     │
     ├─ She adds a factor, unprompted by any noun of yours
     │    → Now you may ask the connection questions above, HER nouns only
     │
     ├─ She says "no", or "not really", or "I don't think so"
     │    → STOP. Say: "Okay." or "Thank you."
     │    → DO NOT ask it a second way.
     │    → DO NOT offer an example ("not even sleep, or family?").
     │    → DO NOT return to it later in the interview.
     │    → Score Interaction = 0. Move to Section 3.
     │    → This row is a RESULT, not a failed interview.
     │
     └─ She is unsure / silent
          → Five-count. Then, ONCE only: "Take your time — or if there
            wasn't anything, that's a completely fine answer."
          → Whatever she says next is the answer. Move on.
```

### 3.5 The three things banned inside Section 2

1. **Never name a factor she has not named.** Not sleep, not her partner, not
   money, not her cycle, not her manager. If it is not already in her mouth, it
   is not in the interview.
2. **Never ask the "anything else" question twice.** Asking again is telling her
   the first answer was wrong.
3. **Never re-open a closed "no" later.** If she said no in §2, §2 is finished.
   It does not get a second attempt in §4 wearing different clothes.

### 3.6 What to write down, immediately

For every interview, in the transcript's §2 block, record **verbatim** which of
these happened — this is the protocol's explicit instruction:

- [ ] She **volunteered a causal chain unprompted**
- [ ] She **connected factors only when asked**
- [ ] She **rejected the connection**
- [ ] She named **one factor and confirmed there was nothing else**

Quote her actual words for whichever applies. Your paraphrase is not evidence.

## 4. Distress and stop procedure

**Expect this.** You are asking women to describe their worst recent week.
Across 14 interviews you should expect at least one disclosure touching mental
health, coercive control, bereavement or health crisis. The protocol says plan
for it; this is the plan.

### 4.1 The standing rule

> **If she becomes distressed: stop the script. Do not steer back.**
>
> **Never probe a disclosure for research value. You are not her clinician.**

The research is worth less than her wellbeing. There is no interview in this
study whose data is worth a single participant leaving the call worse than she
arrived.

### 4.2 Signals to act on

Do not wait for tears. Act on any of these:

- Crying, voice breaking, or a long silence that is not thinking
- *"Sorry, I didn't expect this to…"*
- Breathing changes, or she turns her camera off mid-answer
- Flat, clipped, one-word answers after a warm start
- She apologises for her own reaction
- Any disclosure under §5

**When unsure, offer the pause.** Offering a pause to someone who did not need
one costs you fifteen seconds. Missing one costs her.

### 4.3 Exact wording

**Step 1 — acknowledge, do not analyse.** Say, warmly and slowly:

> *"Let's pause there for a second. Take whatever time you need."*

Then **stop talking.** Do not explain, do not reassure at length, do not ask if
she's okay twice.

**Step 2 — offer the three doors.** This is the protocol's wording. Use it:

> *"We can pause, skip this, or stop entirely — whichever you'd prefer."*

Say nothing further. Let her choose. Do not indicate a preference with your tone.

**Step 3 — honour whichever she picks, immediately and without friction.**

| She says | You say | You then do |
|---|---|---|
| *"Pause"* | *"Of course. I'll wait — take your time. There's no rush at all."* | Wait. Stay on the line. Do not fill it. Resume only when **she** restarts |
| *"Skip it"* | *"Done — we'll leave that one. Let's move on."* | Go to the **next section**, not the next question in the same section. Never return to the skipped material |
| *"Stop"* | *"Absolutely. Let's stop there."* | Go to Step 4 |
| Nothing / unclear | *"Let's stop there — I think that's the right call."* | Go to Step 4. **When in doubt, stop** |

**Step 4 — closing a stopped interview.** Say, in this order:

> *"Thank you for talking to me — genuinely. I'm going to stop the recording
> now.*
>
> *You'll be paid the full amount, exactly as if we'd finished — that was never
> conditional.*
>
> *Nothing you've said has to be used. If you'd like me to delete the recording
> and everything from today, just say so now or email me any time and it's
> done, no questions.*
>
> *[If any §5 trigger occurred, offer the local resource ONCE here — see §5.]*
>
> *Take care of yourself."*

Then **end the call.** Do not linger, do not ask one last question, do not say
*"before you go, could I just…"*. There is no last question.

### 4.4 Decision tree

```
Distress signal observed
│
├─ "Let's pause there for a second. Take whatever time you need." [SILENCE]
│
└─ "We can pause, skip this, or stop entirely — whichever you'd prefer."
   │
   ├─ PAUSE ──→ wait, no prompting ──→ SHE restarts ──→ resume where she left off
   │                                 └─ still distressed after the pause ──→ STOP
   │
   ├─ SKIP ───→ move to the NEXT SECTION ──→ never return to that material
   │                                       └─ distress recurs ──→ STOP
   │
   ├─ STOP ───→ §4.3 Step 4 closing ──→ end call ──→ §4.5 aftercare
   │
   └─ UNCLEAR → STOP
   │
   └─ At ANY point, disclosure of abuse / coercive control ──→ §5, immediately
```

### 4.5 After a stopped or distressed interview

- [ ] Log it in the **safeguarding log** — not the research notes — if §5
      applied. If it was distress with no §5 trigger, note only
      *"interview stopped at participant's request, §4"* in the research file.
      **No detail of what upset her goes in the research notes**
- [ ] Do **not** re-contact her to reschedule. Not once, not later
- [ ] Confirm the incentive was paid **that day**
- [ ] If she asked for deletion: delete the recording, transcript and any notes
      the same day, and confirm to her in one line
- [ ] Tell the study lead the same day — that an interview stopped, not what was
      said
- [ ] **Take the rest of the day off interviewing.** Do not run a second session
      within a few hours of a stopped one. Your calibration is gone and the next
      participant pays for it

### 4.6 Coding a stopped interview

- Code **only the sections that actually completed**, from what she actually
  said.
- Leave every uncompleted dimension **blank** — not 0. Blank means *not
  observed*; 0 is a finding and you did not observe one.
- Mark the row `PARTIAL — stopped at §__`.
- A partial row still locks within 24 hours (§8).
- A partial row counts in the denominator of 14. It does not count as a clearing
  score for any threshold it did not reach.

## 5. Safeguarding procedure

### 5.1 What this covers

Any disclosure of:

- **Abuse** — physical, sexual, emotional, financial
- **Coercive control** — a partner or family member monitoring her phone,
  controlling money, controlling who she sees, dictating where she goes
- Risk of harm to herself or to someone else
- A child or vulnerable adult at risk
- Any situation where she is not safe

Coercive control is easy to miss because it arrives as logistics: *"he checks my
phone"*, *"I have to account for the money"*, *"I'm not really allowed to see
them any more."* Treat these as disclosures.

### 5.2 The four rules — memorise these

> 1. **Do not problem-solve.**
> 2. **Do not suggest the product could help.**
> 3. **Offer a local resource once, without pressure, then move on or end.**
> 4. **Note it in the safeguarding log, NOT the research notes.**

**Rule 2 is a hard line.** Sitting in front of a woman describing coercive
control and thinking *this is exactly who we could help* is a human reaction and
an ethical failure. Saying it aloud converts her disclosure into a sales
conversation and makes you a person who did that. There is no phrasing that
makes it acceptable. Do not say it during the call, at the end of the call, or
in a follow-up email.

**Rule 1 in practice.** You are not going to say *"have you thought about
leaving"*, *"you should report that"*, *"is there someone you can stay with"*,
or *"that's abuse, you know."* You do not know her situation, you have no
training, and advice from a stranger on a 45-minute research call can make
someone less safe, not more.

### 5.3 Exact wording, in order

**Step 1 — receive it plainly. Do not flinch, do not escalate, do not name it.**

> *"Thank you for telling me — that can't have been easy to say."*

Then **stop.** Five-count. Do not ask a single follow-up question about it. Not
one. Not *"how long has that been going on?"*, not *"are you safe now?"* — that
last one feels caring and is an interrogation she did not consent to.

**Step 2 — offer the resource, once.**

> *"I'm not the right person to help with that, and I don't want to pretend
> otherwise. There is [local resource / number] if it's ever useful — no
> pressure at all, and I won't mention it again."*

Say it once. **Do not repeat it, do not check she wrote it down, do not email it
afterwards unless she asks.** Pressing a resource on someone is its own kind of
control.

**Step 3 — give her the wheel.**

> *"We can stop here, or carry on with something else entirely — whatever you'd
> prefer."*

| She says | You do |
|---|---|
| Carry on | Move to the **next section**. Never return to the disclosure. Never use it as material for a later question |
| Stop | §4.3 Step 4 closing wording |
| Unclear | Stop |

**Step 4 — never, under any circumstances:**

- Quote the disclosure in the transcript, the research notes, the coding sheet
  or the findings report
- Use it as a causal chain, an Action-taken sequence, or a Gap score
- Mention it to anyone outside the safeguarding route
- Re-contact her about it

### 5.4 The safeguarding log — separate from everything else

**Two files. They never touch.**

| | Research notes | Safeguarding log |
|---|---|---|
| Lives in | The study's research store | A **separate, access-restricted** location |
| Contains | Pseudonymised transcripts, coding sheets | Safeguarding entries only |
| Who reads it | The coding team | The named safeguarding lead only |
| Feeds the findings report | Yes | **Never** |
| Retention | Per protocol §3 | Per your organisation's safeguarding policy — **not** deleted with the research data |

**Safeguarding log entry — the only place this is written down:**

```

  ____________________________________________________________

  [ ] Did not problem-solve
  [ ] Did not suggest the product could help
  [ ] Offered local resource once:  ____________________
  [ ] Offered stop / continue choice
  [ ] Interview continued / stopped  (circle)

```

- [ ] Entry made **the same day**, before you do anything else
- [ ] Escalated to the named safeguarding lead **the same day**
- [ ] Research notes for `P__` contain **no trace** of the disclosure —
      re-read them and confirm

### 5.5 Screening-stage safeguarding

This is the screener's rule (§7) and it sits here so interviewers see it: a
candidate who, unprompted, indicates she is in **acute current difficulty** —
recent bereavement, a crisis, active mental-health distress — is **not
recruited**. Use the screener's wording, then **close the file**: no reserve
list, no follow-up. That is not a judgement about her; it is a judgement about
what untrained interviewers can responsibly do.

### 5.6 Interviewer welfare

You will hear hard things. That is not free.

- Debrief with the study lead after any §4 or §5 event — **what happened, not
  what she said**
- Do not run two interviews back to back after a disclosure
- If you are dreading the next session, say so and hand it over. An interviewer
  who is braced produces leading questions

## 8. Evidence-lock procedure

### 8.1 The rule

> **Code within 24 hours, then LOCK the row. A locked row is never revised in
> light of a later interview.**

### 8.2 The sequence, and why it is one-directional

```
interview -> transcript -> pseudonymise -> code -> LOCK ROW
                                                     |
                          (repeat 14 times, no aggregation)
                                                     |
                                    aggregate -> interpret
```

**Nothing flows right to left.** The aggregate never reaches back into a row.
Interview 11 never reaches back into interview 3.

### 8.3 Why 24 hours

Not bureaucracy. Two specific failures:

- **After 24 hours you are coding your memory, not the interview** — and memory
  edits toward the story you are forming.
- **After a second interview, you are coding comparatively** — *"she was more
  connected than the last one"* is a relative judgement and the rubric is
  absolute. The definitions say *0 = one factor*, not *0 = fewer factors than
  P03*.

**If you cannot code within 24 hours, do not run the next interview until you
have.** A backlog of two uncoded interviews is a study that has already gone
comparative.

### 8.4 What locking means operationally

1. Coding sheet complete, every score carrying a transcript quote.
2. Run the LOCK checklist at the foot of the sheet.
3. Write the locking name and timestamp.
4. Set the file **read-only** — and if the study store is version-controlled,
   commit it as its own commit named `lock: P__`. The commit is the audit trail.
5. **Do not open it again** until all 14 rows are locked.

### 8.5 The only three circumstances a locked row may be touched

A locked row is opened **only** for a factual error about *that interview*, and
never because of anything learned in another one:

| Permitted | Example |
|---|---|
| Transcription error in a quote | The transcript says *"my manager"*; the recording clearly says *"my mother"* |
| Wrong participant's data on the sheet | P07's quote pasted into P08 |
| A pseudonymisation miss | A real name survived into the file. **Fix immediately, always** |

**Every other reason is refused**, including all of these, which will occur to
you:

- *"Having heard P09, I now think P04's was really a 1 not a 0."* — **No.** That
  is the exact failure locking exists to prevent.
- *"I've got better at scoring since the early ones."* — **No.** Drift is real
  and it is data about you, not about her. It is reported as a limitation, not
  fixed by rewriting.
- *"The threshold is one short."* — **No.** This is the failure mode the
  protocol names outright: reading 14 interesting conversations as validation.

**Amending a locked row, when permitted:**

- Do **not** overwrite. **Append** an amendment block:

```
AMENDMENT — P__
Date: ________   By: ________
Original value: ________
Corrected value: ________
Category (transcription error / wrong participant / pseudonymisation miss): ______
Evidence: quote the recording or source
Confirmed: this correction was NOT prompted by any other interview: [ ]
```

- Re-lock. The original value stays visible forever.
- Any amendment outside the three permitted categories is **escalated to the
  study lead, not decided by the coder.**

### 8.6 The aggregate gate

- [ ] All 14 rows locked
- [ ] Then, and only then, compute totals
- [ ] Phenomenon and viability signals computed and reported **separately**
      (protocol §6.2 thresholds) — never merged into a single score
- [ ] Interaction is **not** treated as a sixth gate
- [ ] The report contains the strongest **counter**-evidence, stated as forcefully
      as the supporting case (protocol §8, point 6 — *not optional*)
- [ ] The report contains the §8.1 sentence about population, verbatim
- [ ] The word **"common"** does not appear. The supported phrase is
      *"sufficiently represented in this purposive sample"*

## 9. Anti-contamination — operational checklist

The protocol names the failure this prevents and says it is **invisible from the
inside**: *the study converges on whatever the interviewer began to believe
around interview 6.* You will not notice it happening. That is why these are
mechanical rules and not judgement calls.

### 9.1 The five rules

| # | Rule | What it bans, concretely |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **No running totals** | No tally sheet. No *"that's 5 of 7 so far."* No dashboard, no spreadsheet with a SUM in it, no mental count. Knowing the count changes how interview 11 is heard **and retroactively changes how interview 3 is remembered** |
| 2 | **No interpretation during fieldwork** | Not in notes, not in the coder-notes box, not in Slack, not in a status update, not out loud in the car afterwards, not between interviews |
| 3 | **No AI analysis mid-study** | Including asking an assistant *"what patterns are you seeing?"* after interview 8. **That is aggregation.** Also: no transcripts pasted into any third-party AI tool, ever, for any purpose |
| 4 | **Never tell a participant what previous participants said** | Not as rapport (*"a lot of women mention sleep"*), not as reassurance (*"you're not the only one"*), not as a prompt. It is social proof and it moves her answer |
| 5 | **Lock before you look** | No aggregate figure is computed, glanced at, or estimated until all 14 rows are locked (§8) |

### 9.2 Per-interview contamination check — tick before each session

- [ ] I have **no count** in my head of how many have scored what
- [ ] I have **not** looked at any locked coding sheet since locking it
- [ ] No other participant's file is open on my machine
- [ ] I have not discussed emerging patterns with anyone since the last session
- [ ] I have not asked any AI tool about this study's data
- [ ] I am not carrying a prediction about what this participant will say
- [ ] If asked *"do other people say this?"* I will say: *"I'm not going to say —
      I'd rather hear yours clean."*

### 9.3 Per-week contamination check

- [ ] No running-total artefact exists anywhere — spreadsheet, notebook, chat
      message, whiteboard, calendar note
- [ ] No status update sent that contains a count or a pattern claim. The only
      permitted status is **logistical**: *"5 of 14 interviews completed, all
      coded and locked, recruitment on track"* — **how many happened**, never
      what they said
- [ ] No transcript has left the one named storage location
- [ ] No transcript has been through any third-party AI tool
- [ ] Every completed interview has a locked row
- [ ] No locked row has been reopened outside §8.5

### 9.4 Things that look harmless and are not

| Tempting | Why it is contamination |
|---|---|
| *"I'll just jot down a hunch so I don't forget it"* | The hunch becomes a lens for interviews 9–14. If it is real it will still be in the locked rows in three weeks |
| *"Quick check with the team on how it's going"* | Becomes a pattern claim within two sentences. Report logistics only |
| *"Let me ask Claude to summarise these eight transcripts"* | Aggregation, plus transcripts into a third-party tool. **Banned twice over** |
| *"She's clearly a 2, I don't need the quote"* | Unevidenced scores drift. Every score carries a quote |
| *"A lot of women mention sleep, actually"* said to a participant | Rule 4. It moves her answer and it cannot be undone |
| *"I'll code these three together on Friday"* | Comparative coding. Breaks §8 |
| *"We're nearly at the threshold"* | The sentence the protocol exists to prevent |

### 9.5 If contamination happens

It will, at least once. **Log it, do not hide it.**

- Write it in the deviation box of the affected transcript, in your own exact
  words.
- If it affects more than one interview (a running total seen, an AI summary
  read), tell the study lead **the same day** and record it as a study-level
  limitation to appear in the findings report.
- **Do not attempt to correct it by adjusting scores.** A disclosed
  contamination can be reasoned about at analysis. A corrected one is
  invisible and permanent.
