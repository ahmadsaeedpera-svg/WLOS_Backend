# WLOS Interview Protocol

**Date:** 23 September 2026
**Purpose:** test whether the interaction thesis is real before building on it.
**Status:** **LOCKED** 23 September 2026. Frozen for Phase A fieldwork.
Amending an instrument mid-study invalidates comparability across the 14
interviews. If something is genuinely wrong, stop the study rather than edit it.

---

## 1. What this tests, and what would kill it

**The thesis, stated so it can fail:**

> When a week goes badly for a woman, the cause is an *interaction* between
> parts of her life rather than a single problem — she is aware of the
> interaction, her current tools address only the parts, and she would value
> something that remembered the context and responded differently next time.

**Five sub-claims, each falsifiable:**

| # | Claim | Falsified if |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trigger | She cannot recall a bad week, or it was a one-off external shock with no pattern |
| 2 | **Interaction** | She describes **one** problem. **This kills the thesis** |
| 3 | Current solution | She already has something that handles it well |
| 4 | Failure | Nothing was missing; her tools and people were enough |
| 5 | Return value | Remembering the context would not have changed anything |

**The honest kill condition:** if most women describe a *single* problem rather
than a loop, WLOS is a worse version of six apps they already have, and we
change the product.

## 2. Sampling — and a problem with the number

**Twenty interviews across six segments is ~3 per segment.** That is enough to
generate hypotheses and **not** enough to compare segments; qualitative
saturation within a segment typically needs 5–8. Spreading 20 across six gives
six under-powered reads and a false sense of comparison.

**Recommended instead — two phases:**

### Phase A — test the thesis (14 interviews)

Depth over breadth, in the two best-evidenced segments:

| Group | N | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 18–27 early career | 5 | Employed or first job, any country |
| 28–35 working | 5 | Employed, any country |
| **Sceptics** | **4** | **Recruited for objection, not fit** (§2.1) |

Phase A answers one question: **does the interaction exist and does she notice
it?** Segment comparison is not attempted.

### Phase B — locate the segment (18+ interviews, only if Phase A survives)

3 per remaining segment for hypothesis generation, then 6–8 in whichever looks
strongest.

> Running Phase B first is the expensive mistake: you learn *which* woman has a
> problem you have not yet shown exists.

### 2.1 The sceptic quota — recruit the objection

Four interviews, deliberately:

1. One who says she **just wants a sleep/period/habit app**
2. One who **uses ChatGPT for everything**
3. One who **would not want an app knowing this much about her**
4. One who **tried a wellness app and stopped** — ask what day she stopped

These are not a control group. They are where the counter-evidence lives, and
the fourth is the only person who can explain day 31.

### 2.2 Recruitment wording

Never describe the product. Recruit on the topic, not the thesis:

> "We're doing 45-minute paid conversations with women about how work and
> everyday life fit together. No product, nothing to try — we're just
> listening. You can stop at any point."

**Do not** say: health, wellbeing, tracking, AI, app, women's health, burnout.
Each pre-selects who says yes and primes what she reports.

## 3. Ethics and safeguarding — read before the first interview

Asking women to describe their worst recent week **will** surface distress.
Across 20 interviews you should expect at least one disclosure touching mental
health, coercive control, bereavement or health crisis. Plan for it.

**Before:**
- Explicit consent: recorded or not, how stored, how long, how to withdraw
- Say plainly: *"You can skip anything, stop at any time, and still be paid."*
- Have local crisis resources to hand for her country

**During:**
- If she becomes distressed: **stop the script.** Do not steer back
- *"We can pause, skip this, or stop entirely — whichever you'd prefer."*
- Never probe a disclosure for research value. You are not her clinician

**If abuse or coercive control is disclosed:**
- Do not problem-solve, do not suggest the product could help
- Offer a local resource once, without pressure, and move on or end
- **Note it in the safeguarding log, not the research notes**

**Research data handling** — a privacy-first product cannot be sloppy here:
- Pseudonymise at transcription; no names in notes
- Store in one named place; delete recordings after coding
- Do not paste transcripts into third-party AI tools

## 4. The script

**45 minutes.** Interviewer talks less than 20% of the time.

Sections 1–5 must complete **before** WLOS is mentioned at all.

---

### Section 0 — Setup (3 min)

> "Thanks for making time. I'm going to ask about how your last few months have
> gone. There are no right answers and I'm not testing you on anything. I'll
> mostly listen.
>
> You can skip any question, and stop whenever you like — you'll be paid either
> way. Is it okay if I record, just so I don't have to take notes?"

---

### Section 1 — Trigger (8 min)

> **"Tell me about the last week that went badly."**

Then stay quiet. Let her finish. The first silence is where the real answer
arrives.

Follow-ups, **only as needed**:

- "What did you notice first?"
- "When did you realise it was going to be a bad week?"
- "What was the first thing that slipped?"
- "Walk me through it — what happened on which day?"

**Listening for:** whether she reaches for one cause or several. **Do not help
her find more than one.**

---

### Section 2 — Interaction (10 min) — *the section that decides everything*

Only ask about connections **using her own nouns**.

If she mentioned sleep and work:
> "You mentioned sleep, and you mentioned work. Were those connected at all?"

Then:
- "Which came first?"
- "Did one make the other worse?"
- "Has that combination happened before?"
- "Is there a pattern you've noticed over time?"

**Critical discipline:** if she names only one factor, **do not introduce a
second.** Ask instead:

> "Was anything else going on that week?"

and accept "no" as the answer. **A no here is the most valuable data in the
study** — it is the finding that falsifies the thesis, and it is worthless if
the interviewer talks her out of it.

**Record verbatim** whether she volunteers a causal chain unprompted, connects
factors only when asked, or rejects the connection.

---

### Section 3 — Current solution (7 min)

> "What did you do about it?"

- "Did you use anything — apps, notes, anything on your phone?"
- "Did you talk to anyone?"
- "Had you done that before? Did it help?"
- "What would you do differently next time?"

**Listening for:** which tools she already has, whether she used them *that
week*, whether she abandoned anything.

---

### Section 4 — Failure (7 min)

> "Was anything missing?"

- "If you could have had one thing that week, what would it have been?"
- "Was there anything you wished someone had noticed?"
- "Was there anything you wanted to say to someone but didn't?"

The third question is the one that surfaces the *unspeakable* part — the
research shows women are less comfortable raising stress at work, and this is
where that appears if it is real.

---

### Section 5 — Return value (7 min)

Still no product description.

> "Imagine next time something similar starts building. What would have to
> happen for you to catch it earlier?"

- "Would it have helped if something had remembered how the last one went?"
- "Or would that feel like too much?"

Then the privacy probe, **before** any product is described:

> "If something on your phone understood this much about your life — your work,
> your sleep, your mood — how would you feel about that?"

Listen for the *actual* answer, not a polite one. Follow with:

> "What would make that feel okay? What would make it feel wrong?"

---

### Section 6 — The employer question (3 min)

Per the correction that this may matter more than acquisition cost:

> "If your employer paid for something like that for you, would you use it?"

Then, **regardless of her answer**:

> "Would you believe it was private?"

Say nothing after. The pause is the measurement.

---

### Section 7 — Reaction, last (optional, 3 min)

**Only now** may the thesis be described, and only in one sentence:

> "The thing we're considering is something that notices how the different parts
> of your life affect each other, and gets more useful as it learns your
> context. Does that sound useful, useless, or uncomfortable?"

**This section's data is worth less than everything above it** and is recorded
separately. People are agreeable to founders. Sections 1–5 are the evidence;
Section 7 is a courtesy.

---

## 5. What not to say

| Never say | Because |
|---|---|
| "How do work, sleep and stress interact for you?" | States the thesis. Contaminates §2 |
| "Wouldn't it be useful if…" | Leading. She will agree |
| "So what you're saying is…" | Puts your framing in her mouth |
| "Most women tell us…" | Social proof pressure |
| "Do you track your cycle?" | Imports the category we are trying to escape |
| "burnout" / "wellbeing" / "self-care" | Her words, not ours |
| Anything naming WLOS before §7 | Ends the study's validity |

**When she pauses, count to five before speaking.**

## 6. Coding — so 14 conversations are comparable

### 6.0 Sequence discipline — data, coding, aggregate, meaning

**Do not compute or look at aggregate scores until all 14 rows are locked.**

```
interview -> transcript -> pseudonymise -> code -> LOCK ROW
                                                     |
                          (repeat 14 times, no aggregation)
                                                     |
                                    aggregate -> interpret
```

1. **Code within 24 hours**, then **lock the row.** A locked row is not revised
   in light of a later interview.
2. **No running totals.** No "we're at 6 of 8 so far." Knowing the count changes
   how interview 11 is heard, and retroactively changes how interview 3 is
   remembered.
3. **No interpretation during fieldwork** — not in notes, not between
   interviews.
4. **No AI analysis mid-study**, including asking an assistant "what patterns
   are you seeing?" after interview 8. That is aggregation.

The failure this prevents is invisible from the inside: the study converges on
whatever the interviewer began to believe around interview 6.

If a second coder is available, have them code blind and compare on
**Interaction** and **Trust** — the two most interpretation-prone dimensions.

### 6.1 The rubric

| Dimension | Score | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **Interaction** *(phenomenon)* | 0 / 1 / 2 | 0 = one factor · 1 = connects when asked · **2 = volunteers a chain unprompted** |
| **Recurrence** *(viability)* | 0–2 | 0 = one-off · 2 = names it as a recurring pattern |
| **Gap** *(viability)* | 0–2 | 0 = nothing meaningful unsolved · 2 = names a specific absence |
| **Memory value** *(viability)* | 0–2 | 0 = would not help · 2 = spontaneously wants continuity |
| **Trust** *(viability)* | 0–2 | 0 = refuses · **2 = would allow, and says under what conditions** |
| **Frequency** *(viability)* | 0–2 | 0 = rare · 2 = monthly or more |
| Existing tools *(descriptive)* | 0–2 | 0 = well served · 2 = has tools and none helped that week |
| Personalization need *(descriptive)* | 0–2 | 0 = generic advice fine · 2 = generic advice would be useless or wrong |
| Employer trust *(descriptive)* | 0–2 | 0 = would not believe privacy · 2 = would |

**Existing tools and Gap are separate and must not be conflated.** *Existing
tools* records what she currently uses; *Gap* records whether something
meaningful remains unsolved. A woman with **no** tool may still have **no**
product opportunity — she may simply not need one. A woman using **five** tools
may still have a **large** gap. Only Gap is a viability signal; Existing tools
is context for interpreting it.

### 6.2 Capture verbatim

- **Her first sentence in §1**, before any follow-up
- **Any causal chain in her own words**
- **Action taken** — the sequence of what she actually *did*, in order

**Why Action taken is its own field.** WLOS does not exist to describe her
problem; it exists to help her act differently. A record like

> *bad sleep -> cancelled plans -> more coffee -> worked late -> called my
> sister -> recovered over the weekend*

shows where an intervention could have landed, and whether she already has a
working recovery path. **If the interviews reveal interactions but no actionable
gap, the thesis is intellectually interesting and not a product.**

### Thresholds, set before the data arrives

**Interaction score is evidence strength, not a market verdict.** A woman may
open with *"I was exhausted"* and only later reveal that work hours, childcare,
sleep and a relationship were interacting. That is real evidence even though she
did not formulate the chain in her first sentence. A score of 1 is a finding,
not a failure.

The signals fall into two classes that must never be merged.

#### Supporting phenomenon signal — does the thing exist?

| Signal | Threshold | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| Interaction ≥ 1 | ≥ 9 of 14 | The phenomenon **appears sufficiently represented in this purposive sample to justify prototype testing** |
| Interaction = 2 | counted, not gated | How *visible* it is to her without help |

#### Primary viability signals — all five must clear

| Signal | Threshold | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| **Recurrence ≥ 1** | ≥ 9 | It happens again — without this there is no return loop |
| **Gap ≥ 1** | ≥ 9 | Something meaningful remains unsolved |
| **Memory value ≥ 1** | ≥ 9 | Remembering context could change what happens next |
| **Trust ≥ 1** | ≥ 9 | She would permit that memory |
| **Frequency ≥ 1** | ≥ 9 | Often enough to support repeated use |

**Interaction is not a sixth gate.** It establishes that the phenomenon is
present; the five viability signals establish whether it is worth building for.
Treating interaction as a required gate re-creates the pseudo-quantitative error
this rubric exists to avoid.

**Wording discipline — do not write "common".** This study deliberately recruits
women who reported a harder week, so prevalence cannot be estimated from it,
only structure. "Sufficiently represented in this purposive sample" is the
strongest claim the design supports.

**Independent kill conditions:**

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Interaction = 0 in ≥ 10 of 14** | Women describe single problems. **Change the product** |
| **Trust ≤ 1 in ≥ 7 of 14** | Longitudinal context is a liability, not an asset. **Kills it independently of everything else** |
| Gap = 0 in ≥ 9 | Existing tools are sufficient. No room |
| Employer trust 0 in ≥ 7 | B2B2C is not viable regardless of employer demand |

**What 9 of 14 does and does not mean.** It means the phenomenon is common
enough to justify building a prototype. **It does not establish a market.**
Fourteen interviews cannot, and any sentence claiming otherwise should be struck
from the output.

Writing thresholds down now is the only protection against reading 14
interesting conversations as validation.

## 7. On the streak — what to listen for

Per the correction not to delete it before finding a better concept, the
sceptic who **stopped using a wellness app** (§2.1, person 4) should be asked:

> "What was happening the week you stopped?"

and

> "Did it ever make you feel like you'd failed at something?"

If absence-as-failure recurs, the replacement concept has to reward
*continuity* or *being understood* rather than *not missing a day*. If it does
not recur, the streak may be salvageable with forgiving mechanics.

**Decide from that evidence, not from the literature alone.**

## 8. Output

One document, within a week of the last interview:

1. The rubric table — 14 rows, one per interview
2. Every §1 opening sentence, verbatim
3. Every causal chain, in her words
4. Every **Action taken** sequence
5. Threshold results — phenomenon and viability signals reported **separately**
6. **The strongest counter-evidence**, stated as strongly as the supporting case
7. Decision: proceed to Phase B · narrow · or change the product

Point 6 is not optional. The failure mode of founder-run research is that the
counter-evidence is present in the transcripts and absent from the summary.

### 8.1 A sentence the report must contain

> This study tests the interaction thesis primarily among working women aged
> 18–35. **It does not establish that this is the optimal WLOS population.**

Phase A concentrates there because that is where existing evidence is
strongest — which means Phase A **cannot** discover that mothers, women in
midlife, women in transition or older women experience the interaction more
sharply. Only Phase B can, and it must be allowed to.

Without this sentence, a hypothesis chosen for evidentiary convenience becomes
a conclusion by repetition.

### 8.2 What good evidence looks like

**Strong** — she exposes the join herself, from a real event:

> *"I thought I had a sleep problem, but when my manager changed my schedule I
> started sleeping badly, then I stopped exercising, then I was snapping at my
> partner."*

**Not enough** — single factor, no structure:

> *"I was stressed because my job was difficult."*

**Nearly worthless alone** — a reaction to a concept, not a report of a life:

> *"I'd love an app that connected everything for me."*

The third is the one most likely to feel like success in the room. It is
agreement with a founder, and it belongs in §7 where its weight is already
discounted.

The pattern to look for, in order:

```
real event -> multiple factors -> recurring pattern ->
existing workaround -> unresolved gap -> desire for continuity
```

## 9. What this does not test

- Whether the *product* works. That needs a prototype
- Whether she would pay. Stated willingness to pay is unreliable
- Which country. §4 of Market Research II
- Whether minors experience this. Out of scope under the 18+ hypothesis
- Whether the join is *perceptible in use* — the largest remaining unknown,
  and only a working Today screen can answer it
