# WLOS Recruitment Screener — Phase A (14 participants)

**Date:** 23 September 2026
**Companion to:** `WLOS_INTERVIEW_PROTOCOL.md`
**Status:** **LOCKED** 23 September 2026. Frozen for Phase A fieldwork.
Amending an instrument mid-study invalidates comparability. If something is
genuinely wrong, stop recruitment rather than edit it.

---

## 1. The rule this screener exists to obey

> **Never screen on the phenomenon you are testing.**

If the screener asks *"do different parts of your life affect each other?"*, then
everyone who reaches the interview has already said yes, and the study finds
what it selected for. That is selecting on the dependent variable, and it is the
most common way founder-run research produces a confident wrong answer.

**Therefore:**

| Screen on | Never screen on |
|---|---|
| Age band, employment status | Whether factors in her life interact |
| Whether a harder-than-usual week happened recently | *What caused* that week |
| Tool and attitude (sceptic quota only) | Interest in "women's health" or "wellbeing" |
| Availability, language, consent | Willingness to use an app like this |

One necessary exception, explained in §4: we screen for *a recent harder week
existing at all*, because interview section 1 has nothing to discuss otherwise.
We do **not** screen for its shape. That distinction is the whole design.

## 2. Quota grid — 14 participants

| Group | N | Qualifies if |
|---|---|---|
| **A · 18–27 early career** | 5 | Age 18–27, in paid work or first job |
| **B · 28–35 working** | 5 | Age 28–35, in paid work |
| **C · Sceptics** | 4 | Any age 18–45 — recruited on attitude, see §6 |

### Spread requirements within A and B

Ten interviews from one city and one profession produce a narrow finding
dressed as a general one. Enforce across A+B combined:

- **No more than 4** in the same broad occupation (e.g. tech)
- **At least 3** in shift, hourly, or non-desk work
- **At least 3** from outside the highest-income country in the sample
- **At least 2** who live with dependents; **at least 2** who live alone
- **Maximum 6** from any single country

These are spread requirements, not analysis segments. With 5 per group nothing
can be compared between them.

## 3. Recruitment message — what she sees first

Posted or sent verbatim. **It describes no product.**

> **Paid research conversation — 45 minutes**
>
> We're having conversations with women about how work and everyday life fit
> together. We're not testing anything and there's nothing to try or install —
> we're just listening.
>
> 45 minutes, online or by phone, at a time that suits you.
> [incentive] as thanks, paid whether or not you finish.
>
> You can skip any question and stop at any point.
>
> To see if timing works, there are five short questions here: [link]

**Banned from all recruitment materials:** health, wellbeing, wellness, burnout,
stress, tracking, cycle, period, mental health, AI, app, women's health,
self-care, balance.

Each of those pre-selects who replies **and** primes what she reports in
section 1.

## 4. The screener

Five minutes. Self-serve form or phone.

### Q1 — Age

> What's your age?
> `[ ]`

**Route:** 18–27 → Group A · 28–35 → Group B · 36–45 → Group C only ·
under 18 → **disqualify** (out of scope under the 18+ hypothesis) ·
over 45 → hold for Phase B.

### Q2 — Work

> Which of these is closest to your situation right now?
>
> - In paid work, full time
> - In paid work, part time or hourly
> - Self-employed or freelance
> - Studying, and also working
> - Studying, not working
> - Not currently in paid work
> - Prefer not to say

**Route:** any paid-work answer qualifies for A/B. "Studying, not working" and
"not in paid work" → Group C only, or hold for Phase B.

### Q3 — Occupation and location *(for spread quotas, not qualification)*

> What kind of work do you do?
> `[ ]`
>
> Which country do you live in?
> `[ ]`

### Q4 — The one experiential screen

Worded to establish that a period exists, **without asking what it consisted
of**:

> Thinking about the last three months — was there a week that was harder than
> usual for you?
>
> - Yes, and I could talk about it
> - Yes, but I'd rather not go into it
> - No, nothing that stands out
> - Prefer not to say

**Route:**
- *"Yes, and I could talk about it"* → **qualifies**
- *"Yes, but I'd rather not"* → **thank and close.** Do not persuade. §7
- *"No, nothing stands out"* → **hold, do not disqualify.** See §4.1
- *"Prefer not to say"* → thank and close

**Do not ask a follow-up.** No "what happened?", no "was it work or personal?"
The first time she describes that week must be in the interview, unprompted and
unrehearsed.

#### 4.1 Why "no" is held rather than rejected

A woman with no standout bad week is **not** evidence against the thesis — she
is outside the population it describes. But recruiting only from women with
recent difficulty tilts the sample toward difficulty, which must be stated in
the output as a known limitation.

Keep 2–3 "no" respondents on a reserve list. If Phase A over-recruits on
difficulty, one of them is a useful contrast interview, recorded separately and
excluded from the rubric counts.

### Q5 — Practical

> - Which language would you prefer to talk in?
> - Would a video call, voice call, or either suit you best?
> - Which of these times work? `[slots]`
> - Anything that would make this easier for you? (captions, breaks, a
>   different format)

## 5. Disqualification — applied after Q5, never explained as rejection

| Disqualify if | Why |
|---|---|
| Works in femtech, women's health, health apps, or UX/product research | Theorises instead of reporting. Will guess the study's purpose |
| Has taken part in our research before | Primed |
| Is a personal contact of the team | Will be agreeable |
| Indicates acute current distress (§7) | **Safeguarding.** Not eligibility |
| Under 18 | Out of scope |
| Asks what the product is, and cannot be redirected once | Already theorising |

**Wording for everyone who does not proceed** — identical, so no one can infer
why:

> Thank you — we have enough people for this round. We'll keep your details for
> future conversations if that's alright, and you can ask us to delete them any
> time.

## 6. Sceptic recruitment — a separate path (Group C, n=4)

These four are recruited on **attitude and behaviour**, never on having had a
bad week. Use a different message:

> We're paying for 45-minute conversations with people who are **sceptical
> about health and habit apps** — including people who've tried one and stopped,
> or who'd rather apps knew less about them. We want to hear why.

One of each, no substitutions:

| # | Qualifies if she says | Screening question |
|---|---|---|
| **C1** | She wants one app that does one thing | "When you use an app for something personal, do you prefer one that does a single job well, or one that joins things up?" → **single job** |
| **C2** | She uses a general AI assistant regularly | "Do you use ChatGPT or similar? How often?" → **weekly or more** |
| **C3** | She is uncomfortable with apps holding personal detail | "How do you feel about apps that keep a detailed picture of your life?" → **uncomfortable / actively avoids** |
| **C4** | She started a health or habit app and stopped | "Have you started a health, fitness or habit app in the last year and stopped using it?" → **yes** |

**C4 is the single most valuable interview in Phase A.** She is the only person
who can explain day 31, and she is the only one asked the streak questions in
protocol §7. Recruit her first; the others are easier to find.

## 7. Safeguarding at screening stage

Asking about a hard week will reach someone for whom it is not merely hard.

**Do not recruit** anyone who, unprompted, indicates she is currently in acute
difficulty — bereavement in recent weeks, a crisis, or active mental-health
distress. This is **not** a judgement about her. Recruiting her into a 45-minute
interview about her worst recent week, run by untrained interviewers, is not
something we can do responsibly.

Response, warm and without diagnosis:

> Thank you for telling me. I don't think this conversation would be a good use
> of your time right now, and I'd rather not take you through it. If it's
> useful, [local resource]. Thank you for your time either way.

Then **close the file.** Do not add her to a reserve list. Do not follow up.

**Screening data is research data.** Pseudonymise on receipt, store in the one
named place, delete when Phase A closes. Nothing goes into a third-party AI
tool.

---

## 8. Incentive

Pay the same amount to everyone who starts, **whether or not they finish** —
stated up front. An incentive contingent on completion buys agreeable answers,
which is the opposite of what section 2 of the protocol needs.

Benchmark to 1–1.5 hours of median local wage, not a flat global figure.

### Stop conditions

- **Group A or B fills without meeting §2 spread** → keep recruiting, replace
  the most over-represented profile
- **Fewer than 3 in either group after two weeks** → widen country, never widen
  age
- **C4 not found** → **do not proceed to analysis without her.** Extend
  recruitment; she is not substitutable

## 10. Known limitations, to be stated in the output

1. **Selection on recent difficulty.** Q4 recruits women who had a harder week.
   Prevalence cannot be estimated from this sample — only structure.
2. **Self-selection.** Women who agree to talk about a hard week differ from
   those who decline. The *"yes, but I'd rather not"* group is invisible to us
   and may be the group WLOS would most need to understand.
3. **Device and connectivity.** All participants have a private device and a
   quiet 45 minutes. The GSMA gender gap — 13% in smartphone ownership across
   low- and middle-income countries — means this sample cannot speak for women
   who share a handset.
4. **n=5 per group.** Sufficient for the phenomenon. **Not sufficient for any
   comparison between groups**, and no such comparison should appear in the
   output.
